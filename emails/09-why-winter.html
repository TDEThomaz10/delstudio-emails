The Long View

Why the drawings start in winter.

Everyone calls in April. April is already late.

{{FirstName}}, there is a rhythm to this work that nobody tells homeowners about. People decide to renovate when the weather turns nice, call in April, and hope to be finished before the holidays. The calendar rarely cooperates.

The reason is not construction. It is everything that has to happen before construction is legally allowed to start.

Illustrative durations. Permit review is the one block on this chart that no amount of money will shorten.

The part that surprises people

You are not waiting on us. You are waiting on the county.

Design and drawings move at whatever pace you are comfortable making decisions. Permit review moves at the county’s pace, and it is entirely outside both our hands. A project that starts on paper in January is breaking ground while everyone who called in April is still waiting on a plan reviewer.

There is a second, quieter advantage. Good contractors book their spring and summer well in advance. Bidding a fully drawn project in late winter puts you in front of better builders with more of their calendar still open — and it tends to show up in the numbers they give you.

If you want to build next year, this is the conversation that starts it.
