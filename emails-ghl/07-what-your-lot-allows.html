The Long View

The rule that stops more additions than money does.

{{contact.first_name}}, people assume budget is what kills a project. In my experience the more common killer is quieter: someone designs the addition they want, and only later finds out the lot was never going to allow it.

Every property has an invisible box inside it. Setbacks pull in from the front, rear and sides. Lot coverage caps how much of the ground you may build on. Whatever you add has to live inside what is left.

The red outline is the only place you are allowed to build. It is the first thing we establish, and it is why Step 1 is a survey rather than a sketch.

This is why the field survey and the plat come before design, not after. It is a genuinely poor use of everyone’s time to fall in love with a floor plan that has to shrink by eight feet the moment somebody reads the zoning code.

Worth knowing about your own lot

— Your setbacks — front, rear and each side. They are rarely the same number.
— Your maximum lot coverage, and how much of it the house, drive and patio already use.
— Whether you sit in a critical area, floodplain or historic district — common enough around here to matter.
— Whether there is an easement running through the part of the garden you had plans for.
— Whether your septic or well constrains where anything new can go.
