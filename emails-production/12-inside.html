Interiors  ·  One house, seven rooms

Kitchen · Library · Sun room · Dining · Sitting room · Barn

Inside

Nobody lives on the elevation.

{{FirstName}}, architects publish photographs of the outsides of buildings, and I have never fully understood it. You look at the front of your house for about four seconds a day, on your way in.

The rooms are the thing. So here are seven of them from a single house, and what had to be decided on paper before any of them could exist.

 

01 — The kitchen

Every coffer had to land somewhere.

A coffered ceiling is a grid, and a grid is unforgiving. It has to reconcile with the island below it and the door openings on two walls, all at once. Set it out wrong and you spend the rest of the job hiding the error.

02  —  The library
Two storeys of panelling, and a balcony you can actually use. The gallery is not decoration — it is how you reach the upper shelves. The glazed room beyond is the wine store.

03 — The sun room

  

04 — The dining room
The coffered ceiling carries straight through from the kitchen into the dining room, so the two read as one space even though they are separate rooms. That continuity is a decision, not an accident.

05 — The sitting room

  

06 — Inside the barn
The sitting room is the formal one — a carved chimneypiece centred between paired mirrors, which only works if the room was set out symmetrically from the start. The barn is the one people remember: a timber spiral stair under exposed beams.

What this has to do with your house

None of these rooms are expensive because they are big.

They are good because somebody decided, on paper and in advance, where the ceiling grid lands, how high the window sills sit, which way you enter and what you see when you do. Those decisions cost the same on a kitchen renovation as they do here. The only thing that changes is the size of the room they happen in.

If there is a room in your house you walk through every day and quietly dislike, that is exactly the conversation to start with.
