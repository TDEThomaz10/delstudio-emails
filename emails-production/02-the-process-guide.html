The Renovation Process  /  01

Most renovations don’t go wrong during construction.

They go wrong long before a single line is drawn.

{{FirstName}}, when a project stalls, it’s almost never because the money ran out. It’s because nobody was clear on the sequence — what happens first, who is responsible for it, and what has to be true before the next thing can start.

So here is the whole thing on one page. This is the same three-step map we walk every client through on day one.

 
01

Getting Started  /  Pre-Design

Finding out what your lot will actually allow.

▸Obtain the property plat and site plan
▸Verify current zoning designation and requirements
▸Engage a site surveyor or civil engineer for the house location plan and boundary survey
▸The architect meets with you to determine scope of work
▸The architect performs a field survey and documents existing conditions
02

Design Process  /  Permit Review

Seeing the house before you commit to building it.

▸The architect develops base plans and record drawings, then design options for your review
▸Once you approve the design and the preliminary budget estimate, the architect develops the Construction Documents for permit review and approval
03

Contractor Bidding  /  Construction

Bids you can actually compare — and someone in your corner.

▸The architect solicits contractors on your behalf for competitive bids, including site visits to answer their questions
▸On receipt of the Building Permit, the architect helps you enter an agreement with the selected bidder and acts as your representative throughout construction

Notice something

The contractor doesn’t appear until Step 3.

Almost everyone starts there. That’s why they end up with three bids that can’t be compared to each other, a budget that keeps moving, and a set of decisions being made on a job site instead of on paper. Getting Steps 1 and 2 right is what makes Step 3 boring — and boring is exactly what you want when a crew is inside your house.
