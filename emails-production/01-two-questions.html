Hi {{FirstName}},

You got in touch about your project last week. I don’t know yet whether we’re the right firm for it — and honestly, neither do you.

So instead of sending you a brochure, I want to send you the two questions I ask every client before we ever discuss square footage, budgets or timelines. In forty years, these have told me more about a house than any measurement ever has.

Question One

What does your family home mean to you?

…and why do you say that?

Question Two

How do you want to feel as you move through this home?

Most people answer the first one in about four words. The second one takes longer — and it’s the one that ends up shaping the whole design.

If you want the one that really gets people talking at the kitchen table, it’s this:

Imagine you and I are talking twelve months after the renovation is complete. The house is a success in every way, and you are deliriously happy with the outcome. What would you have to be hearing people say, what would you have to see happening, or feel in your heart, to know you’d made a great decision?
